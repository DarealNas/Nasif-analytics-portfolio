## **📌 Project Overview**  
This project was completed as a **collaborative data‑analytics group work**, where multiple analysts worked together to investigate academic performance patterns across **478 students** using Power BI.

The goal was to:

- identify the strongest predictors of student performance  
- understand behavioral and demographic patterns  
- highlight at‑risk groups  
- provide actionable recommendations for school leadership  

My role focused on:

- data cleaning

- insight generation

---

## **📊 Key Insight: Attendance Is the Dominant Predictor**  
Across all metrics analyzed, **attendance** emerged as the strongest and most reliable indicator of student performance.

### **Critical Findings**
- Students with **under 7 absence days** are **22.6× more likely** to be High performers.  
- Students with **above 7 absence days** are **15.9× more likely** to be Low performers.  
- **91.2%** of Low performers have high absence.  
- Only **4 out of 142** High performers have high absence.

> Attendance is not one factor among many — it is the central lever for improving academic outcomes.

---

## **📈 Performance Distribution**
- **High:** 29.7%  
- **Medium:** 44.1%  
- **Low:** 26.2%  

### Interpretation  
Medium performers are the largest group.  
Improving even a portion of this group yields the biggest school‑wide impact.

---

## **🧠 Participation Metrics**
Three behavioral metrics were analyzed:

| Metric | High | Medium | Low |
|-------|------|--------|-----|
| **Discussion** | 53.66 | 43.79 | 31.12 |
| **Raised Hands** | 70.29 | 48.94 | 16.84 |
| **Visited Resources** | 78.75 | 60.64 | 18.42 |

### Interpretation  
- **Resource visits** show the largest gap — a 75% drop from High to Low.  
- **Raised hands** is the strongest real‑time classroom signal.  
- **Discussion** is helpful but less predictive.

### Action  
Track resource visits weekly.  
Use raised hands as a live classroom engagement indicator.

## **👥 Gender & Nationality Insights**

### **Gender**
- **Female High performers:** 42.86%  
- **Male High performers:** 22.11%  

Gender independently increases the odds of Low performance by **2.43×**.

### **Nationality**
Performance varies widely (20%–63%), but nationality is **not actionable** — it likely reflects language or adjustment factors.

---

## **📚 Education Stage & Grade**
- Performance improves steadily from Lower → Middle → High School.  
- Grade-level differences (e.g., G‑11 vs G‑10) require **section-level investigation**, not school-wide policy.

---

## **👨‍👩‍👧 Parental Satisfaction**
- High performers: **83.1%** parent satisfaction  
- Low performers: **34.4%**

Parent satisfaction is the **second strongest lever** after attendance.

---

## **🚨 At‑Risk Group Identification**
A specific group of **99 students** was identified:

- High absence  
- Bad parent satisfaction  
- 73 are male  
- Largest female clusters: Jordan (14), Kuwait (11)

This is a **real, nameable group** that can be supported immediately.

---

## **📌 Recommendations**

### **1. Attendance Policy**
Make attendance the school’s #1 trigger.  
Any student crossing **7+ absences** should receive:

- a counselor check‑in  
- a parent call  
- academic support review  

### **2. Parent Engagement**
Launch structured outreach for all “Bad” satisfaction families.

### **3. Medium Performer Lift Program**
Target the 211 Medium performers with:

- small participation goals  
- resource‑use encouragement  
- weekly progress tracking  

### **4. Low Performer Rescue Track**
Prioritize the 99 at‑risk students with:

- case managers  
- attendance intervention  
- parental engagement  
- targeted classroom support  

### **5. Data Improvements**
Collect multi-term historical data to enable forecasting.
